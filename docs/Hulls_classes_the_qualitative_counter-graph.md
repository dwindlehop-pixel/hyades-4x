General Offensive Unit

The most capable Offensive Units were classified as General Offensive Units (GOU). While earlier GOUs contained a human crew, the later iterations no longer hosted any crew at all. Instead, every cubic centimetre of volume was dedicated to weaponry.

There were various known classes of GOU:

    Murderer Class
    Delinquent Class
    Abominator Class

Abominators were composed of multiple subsections. These included at least five major elements[3] - effectively autonomous spacecraft[4] - and subsidiary munitions platforms. These subsections allowed each ship to become a small fleet. An Abominator was only 1.6 kilometres long when operating as a single vehicle contained only by naked hull in full compression. In standard operational deployment mode, it was 2.8 kilometres long. 

Murderers were about 350 metres long;[3] the nose was a sleek point,[2][3] and the rear was blunt and flat.[2]

Three long weapon blisters were mounted on the nose, and another five fat blisters were mounted around the waist.[2][3] All blisters could fire forward, and a target to the side was always in view of at least five blisters.[3]

One example from the Idiran-Culture War carried three effectors in the nose, in addition to three waist effector blisters.[2] 

Rapid Offensive Unit

Rapid Offensive Units consist of little more than engines, weapons and the ship's Mind. While some ROUs are crewed, the crew complement on such vessels is much smaller than those of the more general purpose ships of the Culture, such as General Contact Units and General Systems Vehicles. ROUs are the Culture's fastest ships and have been described as having been outrun only once, by the GSV Sleeper Service, which covertly optimised itself for speed.

The known classes of ROU were:

    Killer Class
    Torturer Class
    Psychopath Class
    Gangster Class
    Thug Class
    Inquisitor Class

Gangsters had segmented cylindrical hulls that were 200 m. long with 50 m. diameters.[2]

The Killer-class was a type of Culture Offensive Unit constructed during the Idiran-Culture War. Units constructed early in the Idiran-Culture War were 200-metres long.

Psychopaths resembled dildos.[2] Based on a 1500 year old example built in the c. 14th century CE, Psychopaths were nearly 300 m. long and relatively slim. The forward section was roughly conical and contained weapons. The central section resembled a spindle; it contained general systems and sensors and a crew section in the rear resembling a thick band. The rear three-fifths of the ship was a cylinder containing the engine.[3]

Psychopaths were considered able to "fuck solar systems" when fully armed.[2]

Thugs had pointed cylindrical hulls with flared rears.[3] Their principle weapons cluster was less than 30 m. in diameter.[2]

Torturers had a weapons pod and a tiny accommodation section in front of a bundle of large engine units; the class had a cobbled together look to it. They contained a cramped shuttle hanger.[1]

Systems Vehicle
A Systems Vehicle, in particular the General Systems Vehicle (GSV), was a type of very large general purpose Culture spacecraft with immense production, habitability, and war-making capacity. Each Systems Vehicle is said to contain the entire sum of knowledge that the entire Culture possessed, could make anything that the Culture as a whole could make, and each ship represented the entire Culture civilization within itself. They were the largest conventional spacecraft built and operated by the Culture.[1]


General Systems Vehicle

The most capable and largests SVs were classified as General Systems Vehicles (GSV). The size range for GSVs was considerable. The Ocean-class was a GSV from at least the 14th century CE - despite much larger GSVs existing at the time[2] - to at least the 19th century CE.[3] The Plate-class was classified as a GSV from at least the 14th century CE[4] to at least the 29th century CE,[5] even when the larger System-class had entered service by the 24th century CE.[1] 

The physical hull of a Plate was over 50 km long and 20 km wide,[1][2] with a height of 4 km.[1][3][4]

The top kilometre was the accommodation section for biological passengers.[3] The flat portions of the top area have a combined surface area of 800 km2.[5]

The middle two kilometres were docks and construction yards for spacecraft.[3]

The lower kilometre was engineering space, primarily for engines.[3]

With an external multiple-layer field-complex could reach 90[1] to 100 km.[4]

One example had an operational mass of about six trillion tonnes.[4] 


Contact Units, particularly the General Contact Unit (GCU), were the primary spacecraft utilized by the Culture's Contact section and they conducted virtually all of Contact's missions, including scientific exploration, diplomatic missions, espionage, and combat. GCUs were not true Culture warships nor did they fully represent the Culture's technological abilities and civilisation, but they were perhaps the most iconic and visible representative of the Culture due to their exploratory and diplomatic role. 


The largest GSVs had billions of sapient inhabitants or passengers.[1] They were likened to be the cities of the Culture because of their high population densities. Most of the Culture lived on Orbitals, which offered much greater space per person.[1][13]

Ships which were accommodation biased had self-sustaining populations that allowed them to crew the ships they constructed. Ship-construction biased - or "throughput" in Contact parlance - ships served as staging areas for off-ship personnel and docked ships.[11] 

GSVs constructed during the lead-up to the war were designed for conversion into direct combat spacecraft. They fulfilled this role early in the war when Offensive Units were rare. Combat GSVs were powerful single-units, but lacked the inherent flexibility of a fleet of smaller spacecraft;[22] very large GSVs were regarded as obsolescent for combat purposes.[2]

Limited Contact Unit

The Limited Contact Unit (LCU) was a smaller, less capable Contact Unit. By the 24th century CE, the Scree-class Limited Contact Unit was the smallest ship of this type. Relatively few were built; they were disfavored as the Minds of the Scree-class were regarded as "guaranteed to go a bit mad" due to the extremely small crew size of five.[3]

Screes were very small; they were the least expensive type of Contact Unit to build.[2] They were only 80 metres long; types of ancient Culture missiles were larger than them.[3] Screes were nearly unarmed.[4] 

General Contact Vehicle

Although not technically a Contact Unit, a related ship type was the General Contact Vehicle (GCV); GCVs occupied the area between large Contact Units and small Systems Vehicles.[4] Relatively little information is available about GCVs but based on Culture naming convention, unlike Contact Units, a GCV would be expected to possess significantly more manufacturing and shipbuilding capacity. For example, the Plains-class GCV was 500 meters in length with 3 hanger bays.[5] It was also designed to transport its own crew of 200 along with an additional complement of 800 personnel.[5] 

The Plains-class was a type of Culture General Contact Vehicle.[1]

The type was relatively new in the c. 19th century CE. An Intermediate bay of a Steppe-class Systems Vehicle could handle the assembly of a Plains.[1] 

Description

Contact Units were equipped with Contact procedures and equipment, which enhanced their information gathering capabilities.[6] GCUs could perform a full scan of a planetary civilization within a few months, in addition to despatching Contact personnel for low-level reconnaissance.[7]

Though not designed as warships, GCUs were nevertheless well armed and capable of very fast travel.[8] GCUs were considered able to "waste planets."[9] 

The Troubadour-class, an early class of GCU, was in service by c. 6th century BCE.[10]

The Mountain-class was a type of Culture Contact Unit active by the Idiran-Culture War. 

Class

Culture hull class is the same as the concept of Hyades load-out. It is the collection of capabilities that a hull's slots provide (`Hyades_loadout.md`).

**Geometry — ratified in a later conversation, formalized in `Hyades_mineral_cost_curve.md` §2:** hull classes do not scale self-similarly. In decreasing sphericity by size: General hulls are spheroids, Medium/Rapid hulls are ellipsoids, Limited hulls are cylinders — chosen specifically so value-per-mineral grows super-linearly with size (the isoperimetric inequality: a sphere has the least surface area of any solid enclosing a given volume, so rounding out at larger sizes compounds with the size increase itself). Sphericity also varies by *role*, not just by size class: Systems Vehicles > Contact Vehicles/Units > Offensive Units, in decreasing roundness. **A General Systems Vehicle is a literal sphere** — the anchor point everything else is measured against, deliberately overriding the Plate-class's literal asymmetric flavor dimensions above (its volume is kept as the calibration anchor; its shape is not). The real dimensions in this document *are* used, directly, as calibration anchors: Gangster-class's 200 m × 50 m gives the Rapid Offensive Unit row its aspect ratio, and Desert-class's ~3 km length anchors Medium Systems Vehicle. Per the same conversation, the fastest Rapid Offensive Units elongate toward a Limited Offensive Unit's ratio; more heavily armed ones foreshorten toward the General Offensive end — meaning hull shape is better read as a function of loadout composition than a fixed constant per class (open question, `Hyades_mineral_cost_curve.md` R-MC11).

Counter-graph

The Culture wiki excerpts above give glimpses of how the Hyades counter-graph will work. The counter graph defines which ship+class dominates which ship+class in combat for an equivalent cost in minerals. **"3 CMY = 2 RGB = 1 Platinum" — reinterpreted in a later conversation (`Hyades_mineral_cost_curve.md` §5):** this is *not* a literal cost or value ratio (it does not mean 3 units of base-mineral spend buys a fleet of equal value to 2 units of super spend or 1 of Platinum). It is a shorthand for something closer to *orders of magnitude* of value — three orders of base-mineral spend sitting alongside two of super spend and one of Platinum spend — without committing to literal powers of ten. The worked example: a negative-mass keel (an exotic component) should improve force projection via acceleration enough that a fleet an order of magnitude smaller **in mass** matches a base-mineral fleet's value. That spec proposes Lanchester's square law as the actual mechanism converting an exotic component's effectiveness multiplier into a fleet-mass equivalence (100× effectiveness ↔ 10× less mass for equal value) — a concrete target for whatever stat such a component grants, rather than an invented number.

A GSV is an easy target, given that it is intended to haul cargo and population. It also lacks mission flexibility since it cannot split, like a GOU fleet of equivalent value. Still, a GSV is easily capable of destroying a single GOU ship, or a fleet of ROU ships. **This last claim is now known to be in tension with pure geometry, not just flavor — formalized in `Hyades_mineral_cost_curve.md` §2.5:** splitting a fixed mineral budget across `N` equal hulls instead of one costs `N^(1/3)` more mineral for the same total volume (a real, measured effect — crushing a solid into smaller pieces increases its surface area at constant mass), so under volume/cargo accounting alone, consolidation always wins and a GOU fleet's "flexibility" has no economic payoff — the payoff, if real, has to come from combat-specific effects (Lanchester's square law's numbers advantage, or indivisibility being a liability rather than just a flavor line) that a Ship Testing Arena (same spec, §6) is proposed to measure directly rather than assume. An Abominator is very resilient to incoming fire because each subsection is capable of independent operation. A Psychopath is very effective against planetary targets.
